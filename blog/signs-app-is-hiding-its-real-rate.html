7 Signs a Money Transfer App Is Hiding Its Real Rate From You

Sep 2026 · 5 min read

Every money transfer app says it has the best rate. They can't all be right — and the only way to know is to look at how many pesos actually land in your family's account.

The good news: you don't need to be a finance person to spot the tricks. Here are seven tells that an app is steering your eyes away from the number that matters, and what to do about each one.

First, the one number to check everything against

Every sign below comes down to one question: how far is this rate from the real one? The real one is called the mid-market rate — the actual exchange rate banks trade at with each other, the same number you'd see if you searched "USD to PHP" on Google. It has no markup and no profit baked in.

No provider gives you exactly that rate for free — they need to make money somewhere. But the gap between what they quote you and the mid-market rate is the real cost of your transfer. You can see today's mid-market rate live on the TrueRate PH homepage. Keep it in mind as you read.

1. “No fee!” is the biggest thing on the screen

A fee is the cost you can see, so apps love to say it's zero. But a company that doesn't charge a fee still has to earn its money, and it often does that in the exchange rate instead, where it's much harder to notice.

Here's how that can play out with made-up numbers. Say the mid-market rate is 62.00. App A advertises “No fee” but offers 59.50: on $500, your family gets ₱29,750. App B charges a $5 fee but offers 61.80: your family gets ₱30,591 (that's $495 × 61.80). The “expensive” app puts about ₱840 more in their pocket.

What to do: don't judge the fee and the rate on their own. Compare the final pesos received.

2. You can't see the rate until you sign up

If an app makes you create an account, verify your email, or enter your card before it will show a rate, that's a sign. A company that's confident in its rate has no reason to hide it from someone who's just comparing. Once you've handed over your details, you've already committed a little, and you're much less likely to walk away.

What to do: get the rate on a public comparison page first, and only sign up for the one that earns it.

3. The rate changes between the quote and the confirm screen

Exchange rates do move during the day, so a small change isn't always a trick. But if the number on the final confirmation screen is noticeably worse than the one you were quoted a few taps earlier, or the app doesn't tell you whether the rate is locked at all, you can't trust the quote you compared.

What to do: before you pay, check whether the app says the rate is locked, and compare the final screen (not the first quote) to the alternatives.

4. The rate is labeled “special,” “first transfer,” or “limited time”

Any of those words next to a rate means you're looking at a promo, not the rate you'll get on transfer number two and beyond. We wrote a whole article on why your rate gets worse after the first send, so we won't repeat it here. The short version: the promo rate is what gets you in the door, and the standard rate is what you live with.

What to do: ask what the rate will be on your second transfer, and compare that number instead.

5. The rate only applies to one amount or one delivery method

Watch for the small print: “rates up to 62.5,” a good rate that only kicks in above a certain amount, or a headline rate that applies to cash pickup while you're actually sending to a bank account. The number in the ad may be real, just not for the transfer you're about to make.

What to do: enter your actual amount and your actual delivery method before you look at any rate.

6. The app never tells you the mid-market rate

A trustworthy provider is comfortable saying, “here's the real rate, and here's how much we add.” If an app never mentions the mid-market rate at all, or claims “best rate guaranteed” without saying what it's the best compared to, it's asking you to take its word for it.

What to do: look up the mid-market rate yourself and measure the gap. The smaller the gap, the better the deal.

7. The rate looks better than the real market rate

This one sounds like good news, which is exactly why it's a trap. A company can't honestly beat the mid-market rate on an ordinary transfer, because that rate is the real price of the currency. So if an advertised rate is higher than mid-market, it's almost always a promo, or it's paired with terms you haven't seen yet, like new-customer-only conditions or a limit on the amount.

This is the same rule TrueRate PH uses behind the scenes: if a provider's rate comes back above mid-market, we automatically treat it as a promo and take it out of the ranking, instead of letting it compete against honest everyday rates.

What to do: treat “better than the real rate” as a warning, not a bargain. Read every line of the fine print.

A 30-second checklist before you send

  • Look up the mid-market rate so you know what “fair” looks like today.
  • Enter your real amount and delivery method on every app you're comparing.
  • Do the math on pesos received: (amount − fee) × rate. That's the only number your family actually feels.
  • Compare the standard rate, not the first-transfer one, since you'll be sending again.
  • Check the final screen before you confirm, in case anything changed.
